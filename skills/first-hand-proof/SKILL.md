# First-Hand Proof

Inject original, verifiable details into content that only your organization could know. This is what makes a post impossible for any other site to copy.

## When to use

- Before drafting a blog post or LinkedIn post
- When a draft reads like something any site could publish
- "Original angle", "unique angle", "personal touch"
- "Nothing no other site can copy", "first-hand", "proof"
- "Could anyone have written this?"
- Refreshing a published post with real experience

**Not for:** SERP analysis (use `serp-check`), final pre-publish audit (use `seo-audit`).

## What counts as first-hand proof

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| A test you ran | "We A/B tested two CTA placements on our contact page for 30 days — variant B converted 34% better" |
| Something that broke | "When we migrated client X from shared hosting, PHP 8.2 broke their custom theme — here's the exact fix" |
| A number from your data | "Across 47 client sites we manage, average mobile PageSpeed score is 61 — here's what moves it" |
| Something you built | "We built a mu-plugin that auto-generates Article schema — here's the code and why" |
| A decision you reversed | "We used to recommend plugin X for caching, then switched to Y after measuring real-world impact" |
| A client story (anonymized) | "A WooCommerce client came to us with 4-second load times; here's what we found and fixed" |

## Method

1. List everything your organization has actually done, measured, built, or learned
2. Match each item to the topic at hand
3. Pick the 2–3 most relevant, specific details
4. Weave them into the draft as concrete examples, not vague claims
5. Include specifics: numbers, timeframes, tools, outcomes

## Quality bar

- Specific over vague: "reduced load time from 4.2s to 1.1s" not "made it faster"
- Verifiable: the reader could check or replicate
- Relevant: ties directly to the topic
- Honest: don't invent or exaggerate — first-hand means it actually happened
